## Busiest Airports Analysis

The aviation industry experienced a severe global disruption followed by a tiered recovery, which becomes immediately apparent when analyzing passenger traffic data from 2020 through 2025. 

Looking at the data, Atlanta (ATL) stands out as the dominant outlier in sheer volume. As a massive domestic hub, it was able to recover much faster than airports relying heavily on international borders, making it the only airport to process over 100 million passengers by 2023. Meanwhile, the visual anomaly of Beijing (PKX)—a sharp drop in 2022 followed by the steepest growth rate of any airport—perfectly aligns with regional lockdowns and subsequent reopening policies. By 2025, international hubs like Dubai (DXB), Tokyo (HND), and London (LHR) stabilized into a tight grouping, illustrating that global international travel has returned to a more predictable year-over-year growth pattern.

Monte Carlo Numerical Integration

The exact answer to the numerical integration of this assigned Weibull Distribution is effectively 1.0. Because the function being integrated is a Probability Density Function (PDF), the total area under the curve across its entire range must equal 1.

The small multiple visualization below illustrates the Law of Large Numbers in an intuitive way. At lower resolutions (n=10 and n=100), the stochastic “noise” of the simulation is highly visible. A single random coordinate has a disproportionate impact on the final result, yielding inaccurate estimates. However, as the resolution increases to n=10,000, this jitter evaporates. The points form a dense, high-fidelity fill that perfectly defines the area under the curve, pulling the numerical estimate tightly toward the true value.

Planning and Prompting GenAI Tools

For this section, I developed a plan to wrangle and visualize the newly provided calcium.csv dataset. Examining the raw data revealed it is in a “wide” format, tracking measurements across four time points (y0 to y3) for two distinct groups (the second group appended with a .1). My plan required generating a subject ID, utilizing pivot_longer() to tidy the data into a single measured column, dropping NA values resulting from uneven group sizes, and then feeding that clean data into ggplot2 for a comparative line graph tracking individuals over time.

Comparing the Prompts: When the generic prompt was submitted to ChatGPT (“Create a data visualization for these data. Calcium.csv”), the GenAI tool completely failed to understand the experimental design. It simply calculated the mean of each column and plotted those averages as independent points, completely ignoring the fact that the columns represented repeated time measurements across two distinct experimental groups.

Conversely, when using my plan-based prompt (which explicitly gave the AI the data shape, instructed it to use pivot_longer, extract the time/group variables, and map them to a line plot grouped by subject), the GenAI output was an excellent piece of working code. The AI was highly capable of generating the exact syntax for the complex data transformation, but it completely relied on me to supply the data-specific architectural logic to make those choices in the first place. This proves that GenAI is a powerful coding assistant, but a poor independent data analyst when left to its own devices.

Furthermore, utilizing ggplot2 to manually simulate and visualize probability density functions made the statistics tangibly click in a way that simply reading formulas never did. Finally, putting this all together in Quarto has underscored the importance of reproducible research, ensuring that my code, visualizations, and narrative analysis can exist harmoniously in one professional document.

Plan-Based Prompt Used: “Using the diamonds dataset in R, write tidyverse code to first take a stratified random sample of 500 diamonds per clarity level. Then, pipe that into a ggplot scatterplot of carat vs. price colored by clarity. Counteract overplotting by setting alpha to 0.5. Add linear trend lines without standard error bands. Format the y-axis as dollars and apply theme_minimal().”

Plan-Based Response Text Generated: “Here is the code based on your plan:


# Load data and add subject ID
calcium_clean <- read.csv("calcium.csv") %>%
  mutate(Subject = row_number()) %>%
  
  # Pivot all 'y' columns to long format
  pivot_longer(
    cols = starts_with("y"),
    names_to = "TimeGroup",
    values_to = "Value"
  ) %>%
  
  # Drop NA rows
  drop_na(Value) %>%
  
  # Extract Time (the number right after 'y') and Group (whether it has '.1')
  mutate(
    Time = as.numeric(str_extract(TimeGroup, "\\d")),
    Group = ifelse(str_detect(TimeGroup, "\\.1"), "Group 2", "Group 1")
  )

# Plot the tidied data
ggplot(calcium_clean, aes(x = Time, y = Value, color = Group, group = Subject)) +
  geom_line(alpha = 0.6) +
  geom_point() +
  labs(
    title = "Calcium Measurements Over Time by Group",
    x = "Time Point",
    y = "Measurement Value",
    color = "Group"
  ) +
  theme_minimal()
